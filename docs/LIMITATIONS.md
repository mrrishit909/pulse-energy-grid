# Limitations (data, forecast and dispatch)

Stated plainly; the site must repeat the first three.

1. **Dispatch is a model, not the grid.** Costs, heat rates, fuel prices, ramp limits, minimum outputs, battery size and VOLL are assumptions (SIMULATED), not market data. Capacities are the maximum hourly output observed in 2021-2025, not nameplate. Reported dollars are costs under those assumptions on one observed day, not electricity prices or real system costs.
2. **Single node, no unit commitment.** There are no transmission limits, voltage or reactive constraints, no start-up costs, no reserves, no forced-outage statistics, no inertia. The IEEE 14-bus case is an illustrative network and is not used in the LP.
3. **The forecast does not use weather forecasts.** Only weather observed before the forecast origin is available to the deployable model; EIA's BA forecasts use weather forecasts we do not have. The `gbm_perfect_weather` variant uses observed target-day weather as a stand-in and is an upper bound, not deployable. Where EIA's forecast is better (see forecast_eval.json), it is better.
4. **CISO's reported day-ahead forecast drifts.** In this extract the CISO day-ahead series under-forecasts by a growing margin (mean bias -0.8% in 2021 growing to -6.5% in 2025, -5.8% on the test period). This is a level effect, not a timing effect. Our model beating it is partly a data-definition effect, not proof of a better forecaster; the `eia_debiased` baseline and the stacked model are provided to separate the two.
5. **Test period covers 2024-09-01 to 2025-08-26 only** (about 12 months, one winter, one summer). Intervals are 80% conformal intervals calibrated on the preceding 14 months; coverage on test can differ because demand regimes drift (ERCOT load growth).
6. **Study window ends 2025-08-26** because NOAA hourly data on the AWS mirror ends 2025-08-27. EIA data to 2026-10 are not used.
7. **Weather is a population-weighted mean of four airport stations per BA.** Weights are rounded metro populations, not demand-weighted. Airports are not city centres; humidity and wind are not used beyond dew point.
8. **Fuel data quirks.** CISO `OTH` includes storage (negative hours), so it is treated as a flat floor in dispatch. ERCOT's sum of fuels departs from total net generation by more than 2% in about 1,990 hours (mostly from Oct 2024; cause not documented in the file). CISO's net generation minus demand minus interchange does not close (mean absolute residual about 1 GW), unlike ERCOT's, where it closes to rounding. These are reported in reconciliation.json, not corrected.
9. **Imputation is limited** to gaps of at most 3 hours and imputed hours are never forecast targets; the 2021-2025 hourly series otherwise keep their original gaps.
10. **Associations only.** The temperature-demand coefficients are descriptive regressions on one aggregated temperature series; the bootstrap CI reflects day resampling at fixed base temperatures and understates model uncertainty.
11. **Reference days are three-month-weekday medians or the 90th percentile summer day** of the test year; they are illustrative, not worst cases. Scenario results on them are not forecasts of any future year.
12. **Two balancing authorities only,** chosen for regime contrast. No claim is made about other regions.

## Additions for release (sources: METHODOLOGY, DATA_SOURCES, DATA_CONTRACTS, ENGINEERING_DECISIONS #21, #22, #25, #40)

13. **The CISO forecast comparison is partly a data-definition effect.** EIA's CISO day-ahead series drifts to a bias of about -6% by 2025 (-0.72% in 2021, -6.36% in 2025, `facts.json`), so the GBM win over it (test MAPE 3.35% vs 7.82%) is partly that drift, not purely a better forecaster. In ERCO the opposite holds: EIA's forecast (2.23% MAPE) beats the independent GBM (4.06%), and the site shows both with equal prominence.
14. **HistGradientBoosting replaces LightGBM** (scikit-learn `HistGradientBoostingRegressor`) because libomp is missing on the build machine. Results would differ somewhat with LightGBM.
15. **The dispatch LP is a single-node, 24-hour model** with assumed costs, heat rates, ramp limits and capacities (SIMULATED). It is not a power-flow model and not a market simulation. There are no losses, no transmission or voltage constraints.
16. **The IEEE 14-bus topology is illustrative** (pglib-opf case14, CC BY 4.0). It is a drawing of the idea of a network and is never the real CAISO or ERCOT grid; the LP does not use it.
17. **EIA data quirks are reported, not corrected:** CISO `OTH` goes negative (storage), ERCO's fuel sum departs from total generation by more than 2% in about 1,990 hours, and CISO's net generation minus demand minus interchange leaves a mean absolute residual of about 1 GW (see `reconciliation.json`).
18. **Study window ends 2025-08-26** because the NOAA hourly mirror on AWS ends 2025-08-27. Later EIA data exist and are not used.
19. **Browser solver parity is defined on the objective** across all 108 test vectors; dual prices are compared only where the vector is `price_comparable`, so displayed marginal prices on degenerate hours can differ between the browser and Python while the cost is identical. If the solver cannot load, the site shows the precomputed result labelled PRECOMPUTED.
20. **Payload.** `highs.wasm` is 1.2 MB gzipped and loaded lazily at S04 entry; fonts are 172 KB. On a very slow connection the interactive solver arrives after the first scenes.
21. **Scenes pending at the time of writing** (S02, S03b, S04, S07, S08): their claims are not yet reflected on the site; see `docs/BUILD_STATUS.md`.
13. **CISO timing conventions change inside the file** (2022-06-14 and 2023-11-01 local; ERCOT has none). The per-series, per-period correction is derived from solar astronomy and the EIA identity, not from EIA documentation (UNVERIFIED); the cause is unknown. Rows within a local day of a change are excluded. Corrections are integer hours, so residual offsets of up to about 0.3 h remain in solar and are within the test bound (+/-0.75 h).
